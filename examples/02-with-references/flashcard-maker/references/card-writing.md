# Writing good cards

These rules apply to every app.

1. **One fact per card.** "What are the two functions of the liver?" is two cards, or one list card only if the list itself is what the student must know.
2. **The front is a question with one right answer.** "Mitochondria?" is not a question. "Which organelle produces most of a cell's ATP?" is.
3. **Keep the back short.** A word, a number, or one sentence. If the answer needs a paragraph, the card is testing too much.
4. **Use the source's words for terms.** If the lecture says "Calvin cycle", don't write "light-independent reactions" on the back without the lecture's term too.
5. **Test both directions for definitions** when the student must recall the term *and* recognize it: one card asks for the definition, another asks for the term.
6. **No give-aways.** The front must not contain the answer, or a word that obviously points to it.
7. **Add context when a front could mean two things.** "When was the treaty signed?" needs the treaty's name.

## Before and after

| Bad front | Better front |
|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | What are the two stages of photosynthesis? |
| What does the mitochondrion do and why does it have DNA? | Which organelle produces most of a cell's ATP? *(and a second card for the DNA question)* |
| The Treaty of Versailles was signed in what year, 1919? | In what year was the Treaty of Versailles signed? |
